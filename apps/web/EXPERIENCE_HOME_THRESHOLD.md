# Home — The Threshold

> The selected concept, designed to one purpose: **arrival.** Home is not a screen you
> read; it is a **place you cross into.** If an employee leaves after five seconds, they
> must carry away exactly one thing: **"This feels like my workplace."** Not a number, not
> a task, not a feature. A feeling of having arrived somewhere that is *theirs*.
>
> This document designs the **arrival moment first**. The rest of the day unfolds only
> after arrival is perfected — and when it does, it unfolds as *walking deeper into the
> place*, never as scrolling through software.

---

## The one job

**Belonging before work.** The first felt thing is *"I'm home / I belong here."* Work is not
the greeter — it waits, calm, in a room further inside, and you reach it naturally. We are
explicitly **not solving navigation** on this page. Menus, counts, tasks, nudges, and
"complete your profile" prompts are *banned from the arrival.* Emptiness is the welcome.

### The five-second test (the only test that matters here)

Close the tab after five seconds. What remains?
- ✅ *"That felt like my workplace. It knew me. My people were there."*
- ❌ *"I had 3 approvals and 6 leave days."* → we failed; that's a dashboard.

Every decision below is judged against the ✅.

---

## What makes a *place* (not a screen)

A screen shows you information. A **place** does three things at once, the instant you enter:

1. **It has a time and a weather.** Real workplaces feel different at 8am and 7pm. The arrival
   *is* the time of day — light, warmth, and words all shift through morning → afternoon →
   evening → night. You can feel *when* you've arrived.
2. **It knows you.** It greets you by name, in a human voice, in serif — the way a place that's
   yours would. Not "Welcome, User." Not "Dashboard." *"Good morning, Priya."*
3. **It is inhabited.** You are not alone in the building. A quiet sense of your people being
   here too — present, arriving, celebrating — says *you belong to a group*, not just an app
   session. This is the belonging beat, and it is the part most HR software forgets.

Time + recognition + presence = **arrival.** Remove any one and it becomes a screen again.

## The arrival — the first viewport

The first screen is the **threshold**: full-bleed, immersive, nothing to *do*. The persistent
nav rail (frozen IA) recedes — dimmed, quiet, not competing. The viewport is the doorway.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓  a cinematic field that IS this time of day  ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓│
│▓▓▓▓▓▓▓   (morning light here; it breathes, very slowly)   ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓│
│▓▓                                                             ▓▓│
│▓▓                                                             ▓▓│
│▓▓     Good morning, Priya.                                    ▓▓│ ← serif, large,
│▓▓                                                             ▓▓│   settles in gently
│▓▓     It's calm today. You're all set.                       ▓▓│ ← one human line —
│▓▓                                                             ▓▓│   the day's *feeling*,
│▓▓                                                             ▓▓│   never its tasks
│▓▓                                                             ▓▓│
│▓▓     · · ·                                                   ▓▓│
│▓▓     (◕)(◕)(◕)  Aarav, Maya and 5 others are already here    ▓▓│ ← presence, low &
│▓▓                                                             ▓▓│   faint: you're not
│▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓│   alone in the building
│                          ⌄                                      │ ← one whisper-soft cue
│              (your day is further inside)                       │   to walk deeper
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

**Composition, top to bottom:**
- **The field** — fills the whole viewport. It reads as *atmosphere of a place at this hour*,
  not a banner behind content. It breathes (a barely-perceptible drift), so the place feels
  *alive* even when still. (Tier-1 now: layered light; Tier-2 later: cinematic photography in
  the same slot.)
- **The greeting** — your name, time-aware, in the display serif. Large, but unhurried. This is
  the page's `h1` (the screen-reader anchor). It *settles in* on load, like lights warming up as
  you step inside — not a flashy entrance.
- **The one human line** — a single sentence about *how the day feels*, drawn from real context
  but spoken as a person would: *"It's calm today. You're all set."* / *"A full one ahead —
  but nothing's on fire."* / *"Welcome back. We missed you."* Never a count, never a verb-task.
- **Presence** — low in the frame, faint: a few faces and a plain-language line that your people
  are here. This is the belonging payload. It is *ambient*, never a roster or an action.
- **One whisper of "deeper"** — a single, soft downward cue. Not a button, not a CTA. Just
  enough to say *there's more inside when you're ready.*

**What is NOT here (the discipline):** no buttons, no quick actions, no KPI tiles, no badges or
counts, no "what needs you," no module grid, no nav menu competing for the eye, no nudges. If it
asks you to *work*, it does not belong on the threshold.

### Time changes the place

| Time | Light | Greeting voice | Feeling |
|------|-------|----------------|---------|
| Early / morning | warm sunrise, gold→soft blue | "Good morning, Priya." | a day awakening, fresh |
| Midday | bright, clear, high | "Hello, Priya." | full daylight, in flow |
| Evening | amber, lowering, golden | "Good evening, Priya." | winding down, warm |
| Night / weekend | deep, dim, quiet, few stars | "Still here, Priya?" / "Enjoy the weekend." | the building is resting |

Context beats the clock: a birthday, first day, return-from-leave, or payday *re-dresses* the
arrival (warmer light, a line that names the moment) — because a real workplace greets you
differently on a day that matters.

## Walking deeper — how the rest unfolds (designed, built later)

Once arrival is perfected, scrolling is **walking further into the building**, never paging
through software. Principles for everything below the threshold:

- **One room at a time.** Each section is a single thing with air around it — *never* a grid,
  never two things of equal weight side by side. You move through rooms, you don't scan a wall.
- **Emotional order, not functional order.** You go *place → people → the day → (only if real)
  what needs you.* Work is the *deepest* room, reached last and naturally — never the entrance.
- **The light travels with you.** As you go deeper, the atmosphere shifts subtly (the morning
  field gives way to a calmer, more focused interior). Movement reinforces *progression through
  a space.*
- **Nothing shouts.** No section earns attention by being bigger/brighter than belonging. If a
  thing truly needs you, it's a quiet, dignified room deeper in — not a red badge at the door.

```
   THRESHOLD  (you've arrived — belonging)          ← viewport 1, perfected first
       │
       ▼  walk deeper
   YOUR PEOPLE  (the place is inhabited; one human moment to notice)
       │
       ▼
   TODAY  (the day's shape, told gently — what's happening, not a checklist)
       │
       ▼
   WHAT NEEDS YOU  (work — last, calm, and only when it's real)
       │
       ▼
   A QUIET CLOSE  (you're held; come back tomorrow)
```

This sequence is the *plan* for the day's unfolding. **We build and perfect the THRESHOLD
viewport first**, validate the five-second test, and only then add each deeper room — one at a
time, each held to "does this still feel like a place, not software?"

## Motion, mobile, accessibility, data — the non-negotiables

- **Motion = atmosphere, not animation.** The field breathes slowly; the greeting settles in
  like warming light. Everything respects `prefers-reduced-motion` (the breathing stills; the
  greeting simply appears). Never a loader-y, app-y entrance.
- **Mobile is the *purer* threshold.** The phone opens the same door, full-screen — arrival owns
  the entire first view, the day is a scroll deeper. If anything, the discipline is *stricter* on
  mobile: one greeting, one human line, presence, nothing else.
- **Accessibility is part of the welcome.** The greeting is a real `h1` (anchor). The atmospheric
  field is `aria-hidden`. Greeting/line/presence text is real text on a controlled-luminance
  zone (AA), never baked into an image. Presence faces are decorative; the line carries the
  meaning for screen readers.
- **Frozen data, no new endpoints.** Arrival is composed from what we already have:
  `/ess/home` → name, `context` (birthday / first-day / back-from-leave / payday / holiday),
  and the people for presence; `resolveGreeting()` already turns time + context into the
  greeting. Anything about *work* (`/ess/signals`) is deliberately **not** read on the
  threshold — it belongs to the "what needs you" room, deeper in.
- **Performance is calm.** The arrival must paint fast and quiet — no heavy assets blocking the
  greeting. Atmosphere is CSS now; photography later loads behind the same slot without shifting
  the greeting.

---

## Definition of done — the arrival

The threshold is finished when **all** are true:

1. The first thing the eye meets is *atmosphere + your name*, not a single piece of work.
2. There is **nothing to do** on the first screen — and that feels like generosity, not a gap.
3. A real employee, five seconds in, would say *"this feels like my workplace"* — unprompted, in
   their own words.
4. Remove the atmosphere and the *belonging* gets weaker (it's reinforcement, not decoration).
5. It is calm, fast, AA-accessible, reduced-motion-safe, and identical in soul on phone and
   desktop.

Only when the arrival passes all five do we begin building the rooms deeper inside.
